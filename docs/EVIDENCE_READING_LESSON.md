## Lesson

1. SOURCE: identify the object and cite its scholarly catalogue.
2. READ: preserve the published transliteration, damage markers and uncertainty.
3. COMPARE: consult the image, dictionary and grammar. Distinguish independent
   evidence from revisions or reproductions of the same edition.
4. VERIFY: label each claim supported, inconclusive or untested. Opening a
   photograph does not establish that its signs have been read.
5. RECORD: retain sources, observations, limitations and the next useful check.
6. SEPARATE: label Tuuwanasavi cipher outputs as modern experiments, separate
   from historical translation. A reversible mapping does not prove ancient intent.

Never turn an incomplete test into PASS. Say what was checked in plain English.

## Worked example: CDLI P405991

- Object: Sumerian grain receipt, SM 1899.02.228, Harvard Museum of the Ancient
  Near East. CDLI marks Girsu provenance uncertain.
- Published reverse line 1: `szu ba-ti`; English: “received.”
- ePSD2's compound `šu teŋ` means accept/receive. This supports interpretation;
  it is not an independent reading of this tablet's wedges.
- Project-run photograph inspection: reverse layout consistent with the
  edition, individual upper-line wedges not confidently resolved.
- Outcome: source-supported translation; independent sign verification
  inconclusive. Cipher untested because no explicit rule was applied.
- Next useful evidence: a clearer reverse photograph or independent scholarly
  handcopy/collation. Enlarging pixels alone cannot recover absent detail.
